01

Nature-oriented / Park-adjacent

Parkshore

Madison Park  ·  1630 43rd Ave E, Seattle WA 98112
Nonprofit Full CCRC Waitlist Closed
Established1963
OwnershipNonprofit — Transforming Age (multi-site nonprofit)
Care levelsIL · AL · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing · Rehab
Full continuum on-site
Entry feeFrom ~$489,000 (varies by unit size, floor, view, occupancy) †
RefundPercentage refundable — exact terms not published; confirm directly (industry-typical 60–80%) †
Monthly~$3,200–$5,455+; avg ~$5,330 [1]. Includes utilities, dining credit, housekeeping, transportation
Websitetransformingage.org/community/parkshore
Phone(206) 539-1915
Notes on costs Waitlist currently closed as of 2023. Entry fee among the highest on this list, reflecting waterfront Madison Park location. Tax deduction ~6% of monthly fee as medical expense prepayment.
Character & Culture

Serene, deeply casual, first-name community. Residents are long-time Madison Park locals who value waterfront access above all. Reviewers cite a "community table" ethos — strangers invited in at meals. Strong fitness culture. Genuinely feels like coming home rather than moving to a facility. One resident has lived here 10 years and calls it "my last move." Has a kayak dock. Closest Seattle equivalent to island life for someone coming from an outdoor-oriented lifestyle.

The Hearthstone

Green Lake  ·  6720 East Green Lake Way N, Seattle WA 98103
Nonprofit ⚠ No On-site SNF since 4/2025
Established1966
OwnershipNonprofit, independent
Care levelsIL · AL · Cognitive/Memory Support
On-site skilled nursing ended April 4, 2025 — SNF coordinated off-campus
Entry feeFrom $195,000 (Lakeside building); Cove East and Cove West higher. Second-person fee: +5% of entry fee. AL community fee: $12,500 one-time †
RefundNot publicly specified — confirm with community
MonthlyFrom $2,795 (Lakeside); Cove buildings higher. Avg ~$5,533 [2]. AL care fees additional, vary by needs
Websitehearthstone.org
Phone(206) 517-2216 (Sales & Marketing)
Notes on costs Entry fee used for capital improvements and campus maintenance. Meal points program provides dining flexibility.
Character & Culture

Down-to-earth, community-focused, multi-generational neighborhood feel. Three-building campus directly on Green Lake's 2.8-mile loop path — one resident describes their dog as "SO happy living just one block from Green Lake park." Village-like scale, less institutional than high-rises. Attracts people who want belonging and nature over prestige. Some couples have met and married here. Long-term residents describe true community bonds. The SNF closure (April 2025) is a meaningful drawback for those who want full aging-in-place assurance on campus.

Websitethekenney.org

Character & Culture

Campus-style complex with an actual arboretum, gardens, and park-like private grounds. Described as "like having a park in your backyard" — with Lincoln Park, Alki Beach, and Fauntleroy Ferry terminal right outside. Charming, historic West Seattle neighborhood feel. Bus line access. Guest rooms available. Very pet-friendly culture. P-patches (community garden plots) on grounds. Residents and families love the warmth and setting. However, the 2024 receivership is a significant red flag currently under management turnaround.

02

Neighborhood Village / Low-key Community

Bayview

Lower Queen Anne  ·  11 W Aloha St, Seattle WA 98119
Nonprofit SNF Off-campus
Established1961
OwnershipNonprofit, independent (volunteer board of trustees). Methodist-affiliated heritage, fully non-denominational in practice.
Care levelsIL · AL · Memory Care. SNF via off-campus partnership with Kline Galland. Intergenerational Children's Center on-site.
Entry feeThree plans: (1) Standard with 60% refund; (2) Declining-to-zero over 5 years (~65% of standard price); (3) No entry fee (higher monthly). 60-day trial period with full refund on any plan. †
RefundUp to 60% (standard plan). Full refund during 60-day trial. †
Monthly~$2,600–$7,592; avg ~$5,096–$5,931 [3]. Includes 30 prepaid days + 10% healthcare service discount
Websitebayviewseattle.org
Phone(206) 284-7330
Notes on costs Parking $200/mo when available. Pet deposit $1,000 non-refundable. Foundation supports residents who outlive resources. No-entry-fee rental option makes it accessible without large capital commitment.
Character & Culture

Known above all for warmth — consistently described as feeling like family. 360° views from 10th floor, private terrace lawn, 56-ft saltwater therapy pool, dog park, and a unique on-site intergenerational children's center that brings young energy to the building. 10-story tower but intimate in scale. Near Myrtle Edwards Park and the Elliott Bay Trail for waterfront walks without leaving the neighborhood. Particularly praised for compassionate staff. Rated 4.5★ across reviews. No pretension — genuine community.

University House Wallingford

Wallingford  ·  4400 Stone Way N, Seattle WA 98103
For-profit (Era Living) IL + AL Only — Not a Full CCRC
Established1993
OwnershipFor-profit — Era Living (8-community Seattle chain). Partnership with UW Retirement Association.
Care levelsIL · AL only
No memory care. No skilled nursing on-site. Not a full CCRC — limited continuum of care.
Entry feeNone — monthly rental model only
RefundN/A (no entry fee)
MonthlyIL from ~$5,215/mo [4]. AL varies (area avg $7,420–$7,880). Includes chef-prepared meals, weekly housekeeping, linen service, scheduled transportation, wellness center with RN.
Websiteeraliving.com/university-house-wallingford
Phone(206) 633-4700
Notes on costs Higher monthly than comparable entry-fee communities because no buy-in to offset costs. Not suitable for those who anticipate needing memory care or skilled nursing without relocating.
Character & Culture

Intellectually flavored neighborhood retirement living — not a true CCRC. Steps from Wallingford shops, cafés, movie theater, and library. Academic culture via UW Retirement Association tie-in: lifelong learning emphasis, lectures, cultural events. Nearly 200 enrichment events/month. Cozy at 146 units. Well-reviewed for friendliness (9.3/10 Seniorly). Best for someone who values a walkable neighborhood, academic-minded peers, and no buy-in complexity — but who doesn't yet need or anticipate needing skilled nursing.

03

Urban Active / Culturally Engaged

Mirabella

South Lake Union  ·  116 Fairview Ave N, Seattle WA 98109
Nonprofit Full CCRC
Established2008 (incorporated 2004; opened 2008–2009). LEED Gold certified 12-story tower.
OwnershipNonprofit — Pacific Retirement Services (Medford OR; 40+ communities in 7 states). All-local board including resident members.
Care levelsIL · AL (Residential Living) · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing · Rehab. Full continuum, all private suites. Virginia Mason Franciscan Health clinic on-site. Medicare 5-star rated (2024 U.S. News Best Nursing Homes).
Entry feeNot publicly listed — call for quote. Most residences: up to 80% refundable. Limited options at 50% or non-refundable. Waitlist: $1,000 Social Membership (fully refundable); $10,000 Priority Membership (fully refundable, priority access). †
RefundUp to 80–90% refundable (80% standard; some units 90%) †
Monthly~$2,495–$7,592; avg ~$5,044 [5]. Includes utilities, cable, weekly housekeeping, valet parking with EV charging, 24-hr concierge, four dining venues.
Websitemirabellaseattle.org
Phone(206) 254-1400
Notes on costs Average monthly (~$6,641) is the highest on this list by some measures, reflecting the SLU location and premium amenities. Entrance fee options with lower refundability trade upfront cost for lower monthly.
Character & Culture

Deliberately casual Seattle vibe — "do-your-own-thing" ethos mirroring SLU's innovation-district energy. Residents described as "accomplished innovators, artists, musicians, foodies, and adventurers." Ukulele group, drawing classes, lectures, lifelong learning. Five-minute walk to Lake Union Park, Pike Place, SAM, Benaroya Hall. Four dining venues including 10th-floor lakeview room. Woodworking shop, art studio, indoor pool, personal trainers. The most architecturally polished building on this list. Attracts globally-minded, active people who want urban convenience with a full care safety-net. Residents serve on committees and council.

Aljoya Thornton Place

Northgate / Thornton Place  ·  450 NE 100th St, Seattle WA 98125
For-profit (Era Living) Full CCRC
Established~2010. 143 upscale units in urban-village setting adjacent to Link Light Rail.
OwnershipFor-profit — Era Living
Care levelsIL · AL · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing
Full CCRC continuum
Entry feeFrom ~$165,000 [6] (corroborated at ~$220,000 by separate source [7]). Options: standard (80% refundable) or 100% refundable (higher entry fee). No-entry-fee rental model also available at higher monthly.
Refund80% refundable (standard); 100% refundable option available at higher entry fee. Refund paid after apartment re-leased.
MonthlyFrom ~$3,690 [6]; avg ~$4,950 (below Seattle CCRC avg of ~$5,750). Includes chef-prepared dining, weekly housekeeping, scheduled transportation. 24-hour dining flexibility.
Websiteeraliving.com/aljoya-thornton-place
Phone(206) 536-2704
Notes on costs Most affordable full CCRC on this list by average monthly cost. 100% refundable option is unusual and worth understanding. Era Living's for-profit structure means no hardship fund if resources are exhausted.
Character & Culture

Upscale but not stuffy. Set within Thornton Place mixed-use development — restaurants, cinema, Thornton Creek trail, and Link Light Rail all at the doorstep. "Aljoya" means happiness. Active programming (~200 events/month), saltwater pool, full-service restaurant. Attracts active retirees who want new-construction quality, urban walkability, and transit access at a lower price point than the downtown towers. Newer construction means fresher apartments. Strong reviews (8.9/10 Seniorly). Northgate location is further from the traditional Seattle senior living cluster but well-connected by rail.

04

Intellectual / Activist / Progressive

Horizon House

First Hill  ·  900 University St, Seattle WA 98101
Nonprofit Full CCRC 1–5 Yr Waitlist
Established1961. ~600 current residents. 33-story West Tower expansion underway, opening 2029 (~198 new residences).
OwnershipNonprofit, independent. Residents' Council plans and runs all programming. Residents Assistance Program (confidential financial support for those who outlive funds).
Care levelsIL · AL ("Supported Living") · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing. Full continuum. Virginia Mason Medical Center 0.1 miles away.
Entry feeFrom ~$64,000 (lowest, 2025) — notably lower than comparable nonprofits. Fee is needs/apartment-based and ranges widely. †
RefundPercentage refundable varies by contract — confirm at inquiry
Monthly~$2,104–$7,592; avg ~$4,848 [8] (below city average — deliberate accessibility commitment). Includes dining credit.
Websitehorizonhouse.org
Phone(206) 624-3700
Notes on costs Waitlist: ~400 people; 1–5 year wait for preferred units. Lowest entry fee of any full nonprofit CCRC on this list. Monthly dining credit model rather than all-inclusive.
Character & Culture

The most distinctive culture on this list. Since 1961 has drawn artists, professors, independent thinkers, and social justice advocates. Residents plan and run all 70+ programs — environmental policy groups, secular philosophy, woodworking, political debates, advocacy work. Staff describe residents as "smart, energized, and active." The building itself described as somewhat dated ("dowdy") but the West Tower will address this by 2029. Lower entry cost than comparable nonprofits — a deliberate accessibility commitment reflecting progressive values. One visitor noted residents "dressed like they were going to church" — aspirational, engaged social culture. Not quiet or low-key.

05

Resort / Luxury / Premium Amenities

Skyline

First Hill  ·  725 9th Ave, Seattle WA 98104
Nonprofit Full CCRC
Established~2005. Two interconnected towers. 2026 U.S. News Best Senior Living award.
OwnershipNonprofit — Transforming Age
Care levelsIL · AL · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing · Rehab. Full CCRC. Unique: Seattle's only Type A Life Care contract — monthly fee stays predictable across all care levels. Type B (modified) also offered.
Entry feeNot publicly listed — call for quote. One published example: ~$1,049,000 for a 2BR/2BA (Cascade Tower); corroborated by user review citing ~$1M [9]. Varies widely by unit. Up to 80% refundable.
RefundUp to 80% refundable †
MonthlyIL ~$2,600–$7,592. AL rates start just under $6,000/mo [9]. Type A contract: monthly fee does NOT escalate when moving to AL or SNF. Valet parking included. Care fund supports residents who outlive resources.
Websiteskylineseattle.org
Phone(206) 207-0036
Notes on costs Highest entry fees on this list. However, the Type A Life Care contract provides genuine long-term cost certainty — a financially meaningful differentiator for those expecting health needs to increase.
Character & Culture

The most amenity-dense community on this list. Five restaurant venues (Columbia Café, Bistro 725, Olympic Dining Room, Sound Bite, Sound Bar), 240-seat Performing Arts Center, rooftop garden with dog walk, 1,800 sq ft fitness center, valet parking. Culture is welcoming and inclusive — cliques explicitly discouraged, newcomers helped to integrate. Residents include retired professors and arts professionals. Sweeping views of Puget Sound and Olympic Mountains. Some negative reviews cite understaffing in the health services wing (The Terraces). Best for couples or individuals who prioritize financial predictability, hotel-level services, and the security of knowing monthly costs won't spike if health needs increase. The Type A contract is unique in Seattle.
