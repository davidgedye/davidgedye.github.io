# Seattle Continuing Care Retirement Communities — Comparison

*Data compiled June 2026. Prices directional; verify all figures directly with communities before any commitment.*
*Last updated: initial version*

---

## How to read this table

- **Entry fee**: one-time buy-in required at move-in for most communities
- **Refund**: percentage of entry fee returned to resident or estate when they leave
- **Monthly**: ongoing fee covering residence, dining, services (varies by unit size and care level)
- **Care levels**: IL = Independent Living · AL = Assisted Living · MC = Memory Care · SNF = Skilled Nursing · Rehab = Rehabilitation

## Group 2: Neighborhood village / Low-key community

### Bayview
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Neighborhood** | Lower Queen Anne — 11 W Aloha St, Seattle WA 98119 |
| **Established** | 1961 |
| **Ownership** | Nonprofit, independent (volunteer board of trustees). Methodist-affiliated heritage, fully non-denominational in practice. |
| **Care levels** | IL · AL · Memory Care. SNF via off-campus partnership with Kline Galland. Also offers intergenerational Children's Center on-site. |
| **Entry fee** | Varies by unit. Three plans: (1) Standard with 60% refund; (2) Declining-to-zero over 5 years (priced ~65% of standard); (3) No entry fee (higher monthly). |
| **Refund** | Up to 60% refundable (standard plan). 60-day trial period with full refund on any plan. |
| **Monthly fee** | ~$2,600–$7,592; average ~$5,096–$5,931. Includes 30 prepaid days and 10% healthcare service discount. |
| **Notes on costs** | Parking $200/mo when available. Pet deposit $1,000 non-refundable. Foundation supports residents who outlive resources. No-entry-fee rental option makes it accessible without large capital commitment. |
| **Vibe** | Known above all for warmth — consistently described as feeling like family. 360° views from 10th floor, private terrace lawn, 56-ft saltwater therapy pool, dog park, and a unique on-site intergenerational children's center that brings young energy to the building. 10-story tower but intimate in scale. Near Myrtle Edwards Park and the Elliott Bay Trail for waterfront walks without leaving the neighborhood. Particularly praised for compassionate staff. Rated 4.5★ across reviews. No pretension — genuine community. |
| **Website** | https://bayviewseattle.org |
| **Tour phone** | (206) 284-7330 |

## Group 3: Urban active / Culturally engaged

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Neighborhood** | South Lake Union — 116 Fairview Ave N, Seattle WA 98109 |
| **Established** | 2008 (first incorporated 2004; opened 2008–2009) |
| **Ownership** | Nonprofit (Pacific Retirement Services, Medford OR — manages 40+ communities in 7 states). LEED Gold certified 12-story tower. All-local board including resident members. |
| **Care levels** | IL · AL (Residential Living) · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing · Rehab. Full continuum, all private suites. Virginia Mason Franciscan Health clinic operates on-site for primary care, lab, podiatry. Medicare 5-star rated (2024 U.S. News Best Nursing Homes for short-term rehab and long-term care). |
| **Entry fee** | Not publicly listed — call for quote. Most residences: up to 80% refundable. Limited options at 50% or non-refundable. |
| **Refund** | Up to 80–90% refundable (80% standard; some units offer 90%). Wait list: $1,000 Social Membership deposit (fully refundable); $10,000 Priority Membership deposit (fully refundable, priority access). |

Residents described as "accomplished innovators, artists, musicians, foodies, and adventurers." Ukulele group, drawing classes, lectures, lifelong learning. Five-minute walk to Lake Union Park, Pike Place, SAM, Benaroya Hall. Four dining venues including 10th-floor lakeview room. Woodworking shop, art studio, indoor pool, personal trainers. The most architecturally polished building on this list. Attracts globally-minded, active people who want urban convenience with a full care safety-net. Residents serve on committees and council. |

## Group 4: Intellectual / Activist / Progressive

### Horizon House
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Neighborhood** | First Hill — 900 University St, Seattle WA 98101 |
| **Established** | 1961 |
| **Ownership** | Nonprofit, independent. ~600 current residents. 33-story West Tower expansion underway, opening 2029 (~198 new residences). Residents' Council plans and runs all programming. Residents Assistance Program (confidential financial support for those who outlive funds). |
| **Care levels** | IL · AL (called "Supported Living") · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing. Full CCRC continuum. Virginia Mason Medical Center 0.1 miles away. |
| **Entry fee** | From ~$64,000 (lowest, 2025) — notably lower than comparable nonprofits. Fee is needs/apartment-based and ranges widely. |
| **Refund** | Percentage refundable varies by contract — confirm at inquiry |
| **Monthly fee** | ~$2,104–$7,592; average ~$4,848 (below city average — deliberate accessibility commitment). Includes dining credit. |
| **Notes on costs** | Waitlist: ~400 people; 1–5 year wait for preferred units. Lowest entry fee of any full nonprofit CCRC on this list. Monthly dining credit model rather than all-inclusive. |
| **Vibe** | The most distinctive culture on this list. Since 1961 has drawn artists, professors, independent thinkers, and social justice advocates. Residents plan and run all 70+ programs — environmental policy groups, secular philosophy, woodworking, political debates, advocacy work. Staff describe residents as "smart, energized, and active." The building itself described as somewhat dated ("dowdy") but the West Tower will address this by 2029. Lower entry cost than comparable nonprofits — a deliberate accessibility commitment reflecting progressive values. One visitor noted residents "dressed like they were going to church" — aspirational, engaged social culture. Not quiet or low-key. |

## Group 5: Resort / Luxury / Premium amenities

### Skyline
| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Neighborhood** | First Hill — 725 9th Ave, Seattle WA 98104 |
| **Established** | ~2005 |
| **Ownership** | Nonprofit (Transforming Age). Two interconnected towers. 2026 U.S. News Best Senior Living award. |
| **Care levels** | IL · AL · Memory Care · Skilled Nursing · Rehab. Full CCRC. **Unique: Seattle's only Type A Life Care contract** — monthly fee stays predictable across all care levels. Type B (modified) also offered. |
| **Entry fee** | Not publicly listed — call for quote. One published example: ~$1,049,000 for a 2BR/2BA (Cascade Tower). Varies widely by unit. Up to 80% refundable. |
| **Refund** | Up to 80% refundable |
| **Monthly fee** | IL ~$2,600–$7,592. AL ~$5,100–$5,800. One third-party source lists health services avg at ~$11,880 — verify directly. Type A contract: monthly fee does NOT escalate when moving from IL to AL or SNF (the key financial differentiator). |
| **Notes on costs** | Highest entry fees on this list. However, the Type A Life Care contract provides genuine long-term cost certainty — a financially meaningful differentiator for those expecting health needs to increase. Valet parking included. Care fund supports residents who outlive resources. |
| **Vibe** | The most amenity-dense community on this list. Five restaurant venues (Columbia Café, Bistro 725, Olympic Dining Room, Sound Bite, Sound Bar), 240-seat Performing Arts Center, rooftop garden with dog walk, 1,800 sq ft fitness center, valet parking. Culture is welcoming and inclusive — cliques explicitly discouraged, newcomers helped to integrate. Residents include retired professors and arts professionals. Sweeping views of Puget Sound and Olympic Mountains. Some negative reviews cite understaffing in the health services wing (The Terraces). Best for couples or individuals who prioritize financial predictability, hotel-level services, and the security of knowing monthly costs won't spike if health needs increase. The Type A contract is unique in Seattle. |
| **Website** | https://skylineseattle.org |
| **Tour phone** | (206) 207-0036 |
